History of the Internet

The Internet is a global network that connects computers and devices around the world, allowing them to communicate and exchange information. Its development began in the 1960s and evolved through several important technological milestones into the Internet we use today.

{% endblock %} {% block content %}

Internet Timeline

1969 - ARPANET:

The first major computer network was created by the U.S. Department of Defense. The first message was sent between computers at UCLA and Stanford Research Institute.

https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1971 - Email:

Ray Tomlinson developed network email and introduced the @ symbol to separate a username from the computer/domain.

https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/1971/

1973-1974 - TCP/IP:

Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn developed the foundations of TCP/IP, the communication protocols that allow different computer networks to communicate.

https://www.computerhistory.org/internethistory/1970s/

1983 - Birth of the modern Internet:

ARPANET officially switched to TCP/IP on January 1, 1983. This is often considered a major starting point of the modern Internet.

https://www.darpa.mil/news/features/arpanet

1984 - Domain Name System (DNS):

DNS was introduced, allowing people to use readable domain names instead of remembering numerical IP addresses.

https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc882/

1986 - NSFNET:

The U.S. National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect researchers and supercomputer centers. It became a major Internet backbone.

Source: National Science Foundation

1995 - Commercial Internet:

As commercial networks expanded, NSFNET's backbone was retired. Internet service moved beyond research institutions.

Source: National Science Foundation

Late 1990s–2000s - Broadband and Wi-Fi:

DSL and cable made home connections faster than dial-up. Wi-Fi let devices connect without a network cable.

Source: ITU broadband history Source: IEEE Wi-Fi timeline

2000s - Mobile Internet:

Third-generation (3G) mobile networks brought Internet access to more phones while people were away from home.

Source: ITU mobile history

2012 - IPv6:

World IPv6 Launch helped make the newer Internet Protocol available on websites, networks, and devices. IPv6 provides many more addresses than IPv4.

Source: Internet Society

2010s–2020s - 4G and 5G:

New mobile network generations increased connection speeds and supported more mobile Internet services.

Source: ITU mobile history

Today - Global connectivity:

Fixed and mobile broadband continue to expand. The ITU estimated that about 6 billion people used the Internet in 2025, while many people remained offline.

Source: ITU Facts and Figures 2025
