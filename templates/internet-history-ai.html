Overview

The Internet is a global network that connects computers and devices around the world, allowing them to communicate and exchange information. Its development began in the 1960s and evolved through several important technological milestones into the Internet we use today.

Key Milestones

1969 – ARPANET

The first major computer network was created by the U.S. Department of Defense. The first message was sent between computers at UCLA and Stanford Research Institute.

“All this came together in September 1969 when BBN installed the first IMP at UCLA and the first host computer was connected.”

Source: Internet Society

1971 – Email

Ray Tomlinson developed network email and introduced the @ symbol to separate a username from the computer/domain.

“Ray Tomlinson of Bolt, Beranek and Newman chooses the now-iconic ‘@’ sign for his networked email protocol on the ARPAnet.”

Source: Computer History Museum

1973–1974 – TCP/IP

Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn developed the foundations of TCP/IP, the communication protocols that allow different computer networks to communicate.

“Bob Kahn and Vint Cerf publish ‘A Protocol for Packet Network Interconnection’ in the May 1974 issue of IEEE Transactions on Communications Technology.”

Source: Computer History Museum

1983 – Birth of the Modern Internet

ARPANET officially switched to TCP/IP on January 1, 1983. This is often considered a major starting point of the modern Internet.

“Switch to Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and the Internet Protocol (IP) (TCP/IP)”

Source: DARPA

1984 – Domain Name System (DNS)

DNS was introduced, allowing people to use readable domain names instead of remembering numerical IP addresses.

“This RFC introduces domain style names, their use for ARPA Internet mail and host address support”

Source: RFC Editor

1986 – NSFNET

NSFNET connected researchers and supercomputer centers and became a major Internet backbone.

Source: National Science Foundation

1995 – Commercial Internet

Commercial networks expanded, and the NSFNET backbone was retired.

Source: National Science Foundation

Late 1990s–2000s – Broadband and Wi-Fi

Broadband improved home Internet speeds, while Wi-Fi made local wireless connections common.

Source: ITU

Source: IEEE

2000s – Mobile Internet

3G networks brought Internet access to more mobile phones.

Source: ITU

2012 – IPv6 Launch

Websites, networks, and devices began enabling IPv6 more widely, allowing many more Internet addresses.

Source: Internet Society

2010s–2020s – 4G and 5G

Later mobile network generations improved the speed and capacity of mobile Internet connections.

Source: ITU

Today – Global Connectivity

The ITU estimated about 6 billion Internet users in 2025, though many people were still offline.

Source: ITU Facts and Figures 2025
